Automated Process Control

Multivariable controllers that hold the plant at its economic optimum every minute, not just when the best operator is on shift.

Architecture

DCS → OPC → APC, inside the OT boundary

The APC engine runs in the OT network or the DMZ, close to the DCS. It reads process values and writes setpoints over OPC. Model training and monitoring happen on the IT side, and only validated models cross the DMZ.

Models

The right optimizer for each control problem

Dynamic · fast

Model Predictive Control (MPC)

Predicts how each CV will move over the next minutes for every possible MV move. It picks the moves that best meet targets without breaking limits, then repeats every cycle.

Used forCombustion, bed temperature, steam temperature, ESP, emissions
Steady-state · economic

Linear / Mixed-Integer Programming

Finds the cheapest way to meet a demand across many units with on/off and capacity limits. It works from efficiency curves re-traced from plant data.

Used forAux load allocation, CT/CW optimization, soot-blowing schedules
Inference · AI

Soft sensors & ML models

Estimate what isn’t measured online (coal GCV, PF fineness, NOx ahead of the analyser, fouling) and feed them to the MPC and LP as live inputs.

Used forFeedforward and disturbance rejection in every application
The general objective
minΔu  J = Σ ‖ŷk − rk‖²Q + Σ ‖Δuk‖²R + Σ cTuk
subject to  umin ≤ u ≤ umax,  |Δu| ≤ Δumax,  ymin ≤ ŷ ≤ ymax
  • Track targets. Keep controlled variables (O2, steam temperature, bed temperature) on target.
  • Move gently. Penalize large valve and damper moves to protect equipment and operator trust.
  • Run cheapest. An economic term for fuel, spray, aux power or reagent cost.

Why it pays

Squeeze, then shift

Under manual control, operators keep a wide safety margin from every limit because the process swings. APC first squeezes the variability.

The target can then shift closer to the constraint (lower O2, higher steam temperature, less spray) without ever crossing it. The gap between the two averages is the benefit.

Lower heat rate

Less dry-gas, unburnt and spray loss; steam held at design

Lower aux power

Fans, pumps, CT cells and ESP run only as hard as needed

Stable operation

Less variability, faster load-following on IEX ramps, consistent across shifts

Compliance at least cost

SO2, NOx and SPM held inside limits with minimum reagent

Safety by design

The DCS stays in charge

Operator switch per loop

Each APC loop has an ON/OFF on the DCS faceplate. The operator can take back control at any moment.

Bounded writes

Setpoints are clamped within engineer-set bands and rate-of-change limits. The APC never writes to final control elements directly.

Watchdog fallback

A heartbeat between the APC and the DCS. If it is lost, the DCS reverts bumplessly to base PID control.

DCS interlocks untouched

Protection, burner management and trips stay in the DCS/BMS. The APC sits above them, never in place of them.

Offline validation

Every controller is tested on the digital twin before go-live.

Full audit trail

Every write, override and switch-off is logged and reviewed by the APC Improvement Agent.
